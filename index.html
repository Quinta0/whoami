I volunteered on Brave Frontier Revival, the community-run revival of the mobile game Brave Frontier, while its players grew from 2,500 to over 14,000. I profiled the live, multi-threaded C++ server first, then worked through it in small pull requests: the arena, colosseum and friend queries, a read pool, caches and sessions; libdeflate compression; VAES and AVX2 paths that check themselves against the proven code at start-up; and a per-thread RNG in place of a global lock. The latest round, on the UserInfo and friend paths, doubled what the live server handles before it slows down. Founder’s reference available on request.

  • 2× capacity on the live server, about 550 to 1,100 requests per second
  • 115 ms median wait, down from 220; the slowest 1% about 40% faster
  • +27% throughput in an earlier round, 210 to 266 requests per second
  • 25% less CPU per request in the project benchmark
  • 9.7 s to log in, down from 23 seconds
  • 2.3 s to open the achievements screen, down from 7.2
  • 14,000 players and more, up from 2,500
  • 60+ commits in nine pull requests, reviewed by the founder

The VLAN boundary stops it.

Nargothrond is the server from the last scene, drawing 17 watts in a closet. From outside, a request passes the Cloudflare or Pangolin tunnel into Traefik, which reads the hostname and routes it; the router opens no inbound port. From the couch, devices ask AdGuard Home first, then reach Traefik over the LAN. CrowdSec bans hostile addresses.

  • 0 inbound ports open on the router
  • 6 networks the containers are split across, each talking only to what it needs
  • VLAN a compromised bulb cannot reach the containers that hold photos or passwords

Sorting, narrated step by step.

Insertion sort, recorded once and replayed by your scroll.

VALGO visualises the classic sorting algorithms on a dark, grid-lined canvas, with a commentary track that says what is happening and why. Each algorithm runs once and records every compare, swap, pivot and merge; the player replays that record like a video, so you can pause, scrub and change speed. The bars above work the same way. Next.js, React, TypeScript and Tailwind.

  • 10 languages for the commentary panel
  • 3 growth curves side by side, O(n²), O(n log n) and O(n + k), with a live marker at the current array size
  • 4 colour-coded states: comparing, swapping, pivot and sorted, under a pointer that shows where the algorithm is looking

Wallpapers that move, on screens that last.

Wallpaper oneWallpaper two

Idle dimming: the background fades until you are back.

BenthicBloom is a GNOME Shell 50 extension that rotates wallpapers with a crossfade, plays looping video or GIF wallpapers and protects OLED screens from burn-in. Frames are decoded with GStreamer and drawn on GNOME Shell’s own Clutter stage; playback pauses on battery power or under a fullscreen window. Written in GJS, with a top-bar indicator and a libadwaita preferences window.

  • Shift the background drifts a few pixels on a slow cycle, so the same subpixels are not lit all day
  • Dim the background fades when the system sits idle, and a change is forced after a maximum static time
  • Fix the battery lookup made asynchronous, which removed a freeze at login
